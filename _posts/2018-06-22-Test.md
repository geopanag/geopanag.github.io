##Preconference 

###Brainhack Networks
This connectomics hackathon was not a competition, but rather an effort to come up with new ideas for open problems by putting people with diverse background together for a weekend. The ignite talks were awesome. [Vincenzo Nicosia](http://www.maths.qmul.ac.uk/~vnicosia/) gave a brief intro to complex networks, commented on the commonalities between different types(twitter, railways, brains  etc.) and underlined that to argue about power laws, you need sufficient scale. [Daniel Margulies](https://www.cbs.mpg.de/person/margulies/205351), talked about his research on combining the brain network and its actual geometric space, while during the afternoon there was a quick guest star by [Gael Varoquaux](http://gael-varoquaux.info/)!    
I worked on a project for fingerprinting using functional connectomes, by [Enrico Amico](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ConnplexityLab/research/research-group). The state of the art algorithm was based on removing variance from the functional connectomes using PCA, which in the right ammount can maximize the correlation between functional connectomes of the same subject. Our group came up with several extensions of the methods that actually improved fingerprinting: 
 focusing on connectomes of a specific task / using only specific regions of the brain / using a normalized Laplacian of the connectome as input to the initial algorithm / by directly calculating partial correlation between subjects. I can't help but mentioning another cool project, [101nights](http://dreamsessions.org/101nights.html), on which one can map EEG recordings to actual dreaming sessions.          
The hackathon was a fun experience. Though lacking the competitiveness and heat of a typical hackathon, there were no discounts on productivity nor creativity. I met some very cool people and had the chance to get a good warmup for the conference. 

###SYNS pre-event
The Society for Young Network Scientists organized a rather interesting event, where three well known scientists presented a story about the actual course of a paper before publishing. In this case, we were lucky to have [Yamir Moreno](http://cosnet.bifi.es/people/yamir-moreno/), [Sonia Kefi](http://sonia.kefi.fr/)  and [Stefano Battiston](http://www.bf.uzh.ch/cms/en/battiston.stefano.html), presenting the background stories of three very strong papers on epidimiology, ecology and finance respectively. I think I can summarize the three main ideas:    
- Be patient, some papers take more then half a decade to be published.     
- Don't get discouraged by a rejection.. or 3.      
- Take short steps. A paper's publication may depend on a lot of outside parameters that change rapidly, so you need to be flexible.
